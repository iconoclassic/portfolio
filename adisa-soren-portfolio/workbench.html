Case study · Verily Workbench

Turning a stalled AI roadmap into a funded product strategy

Workbench product managers had 30 AI feature ideas and a strategy offsite eight weeks away. A two-phase study with 300 biomedical researchers showed leadership which capabilities to build first.

Situation

Customers were asking. Competitors were moving.

Context

  • Customers were serious about AI and wanted a clear AI product strategy for Workbench, a trusted research environment for multimodal biomedical data.
  • Competitors were shipping eye-catching AI demos before Workbench could launch its own.
  • The chief product officer asked for a coherent strategy that brings new value to customers, not AI bolted onto the existing product.

Constraints

  • A product-manager-only offsite on Workbench AI strategy was eight weeks out.
  • Product managers had spent months vibe-coding more than 30 feature ideas, with no shared way to rank them.
  • The end-of-year crunch left little time for anything collaborative.

Task

Give leadership a fair way to choose. Which ideas matter most to researchers, and why.

The goal: find which feature ideas resonate most with the people Workbench serves, why, and how to refine them so their value comes through, in time to shape the offsite.

Reduce strategic ambiguity

A framework for ranking ideas

A surplus of AI ideas had no systematic way to be prioritized. The study would provide one.

Target the highest-return workflows

Where the work burden is

Measure the burden and error sensitivity of core research tasks to find the most valuable openings.

Stand out in the market

Table stakes or difference

Separate what customers now expect from AI in research tools from what could set Workbench apart.

Action · before fieldwork

Three steps made an eight-week study possible.

01

Align UX leadership

How do we convince UX leadership to shift resources to this?

As a UX-led initiative, what can we realistically achieve in the time?

02

Win an executive sponsor

The Director of AI and Data Science sponsored the study, which gave it standing with product leadership before any data came in.

03

Cull 30 ideas to 10 concepts

  • Mapped each idea to past user and market research
  • Checked it against verifiable product domain expertise
  • Kept a few aspirational, riskier bets on purpose

Findings

Take the drudgery away first. Tedious work ranked above help with thinking.

The concepts that resonated most

  • AI-enabled data curationClean and transform raw data
  • Data explorer agentVerify, then explore
  • Automated experiment trackerCapture work as it happens

AI use was already widespread: at each of the 12 stages of the research lifecycle we asked about, between 84% and 100% of the 300 respondents used AI or ML tools.

Findings · data explorer agent

Researchers already use general chatbots for analysis. The chatbot limits how far they get.

FrictionWith a general LLM todayWith a specialized agent
Data out of reachGoverned or large data can’t be uploaded, so people analyze samples.Runs where the data lives, under existing access controls.
Copy-paste loopCode, errors and plots move back and forth between chat and notebook.Runs code, reads its own output and corrects itself in one loop.
Scope capped by the promptThe analysis goes only as far as the user thinks to ask.Surfaces outliers, skew and patterns worth a look on its own.
Hard to verifySilent assumptions and no audit trail make results hard to defend.Shows every step and applies domain conventions by default.

Empowerment for some, convenience for others

Less technical colleagues saw a way to explore data visually, gain confidence and stop waiting on technical teams. Technical users saw a time saver, useful but not critical. The opportunity: design for accessibility first while still saving experts time.

Paired with data curation, it checks the AI’s work

Curation cleans and transforms raw data; the explorer verifies the result before exploring it. Before-and-after plots catch dropped rows, skew and broken joins, so people review visible evidence instead of auditing transform code. Together they cover the early journey from raw data to first insight.

Recommendations

Three concepts, in priority order. Then scored with engineering and product in one session.

ConceptWhy it leadsHow to refine it
1. AI-enabled data curationA universal pain point: the drudgery of preparing data. Static “analysis-ready” datasets are too rigid; researchers want curation that respects their own aims.Keep a human in the loop, with side-by-side views to accept, reject or override each change.
2. Automated experiment trackerAddresses the reproducibility crisis, and was rated the most innovative concept. E-notebooks fail because they digitize manual work instead of automating it.Capture in the background with no extra effort, and cover experimental work beyond AI models.
3. Data explorer agentThe check on AI curation that eases “black box” worries, and opens data to clinicians and scientists who don’t program.Move from one-off queries to a conversation that suggests the next drill-down.

Result

From a point of view to a funded workstream.

Executive and strategic influence

  • The research reached the chief product officer, who commissioned a two-week product and UX design sprint to prototype a unified concept.
  • The commercial team ran its own buyer-facing, feature-based TAM analysis. Its results largely backed the recommended AI investments.

Roadmap and product decisions

  • The data explorer agent went on the roadmap as an extension of existing data discovery.
  • The data curation team is scoping how to bring curation features to end users.
  • New investment was approved for metadata curation and MLflow integration.
